Why it matters

The SRA requires firms to publish price and service information for nine specified areas of work, together with a complaints procedure telling clients how and when they may approach the Legal Ombudsman.

A year after the rules took effect, three quarters of firm websites were not compliant. The SRA runs periodic sweeps and has issued hundreds of warnings and a number of fixed penalties. A first finding carries a £750 penalty; £1,500 after that.

What a finding looks like

Every finding quotes your own wording and links the exact page, so you can check it in ten seconds rather than taking my word for it. A real example, anonymised:

Rule 2.1 — complaints procedure Not met

The complaints page explains how to complain to the firm and refers the reader to the SRA, but never mentions the Legal Ombudsman or the time limits for approaching them.

On the firm’s complaints page — “Solicitors Regulation Authority” appears twice; “Legal Ombudsman” appears no times.

That firm was otherwise in good order — its published fees and hourly rates met Rule 1.4. The report says so, because you should not spend an afternoon fixing things that are already right.
