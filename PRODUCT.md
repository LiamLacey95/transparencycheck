# Transparency Check — product truth

## What it is

A fixed-fee check of a UK law firm's public website against the SRA Transparency Rules. £149 per
firm, nothing to pay if nothing needs changing.

## Who buys it

Principals, practice managers and COLPs at **small English and Welsh law firms** — typically two
to twenty fee earners, offices in market towns, no in-house compliance function. They are
conservative, time-poor, and highly allergic to being sold to. They have seen a great many cold
emails claiming their website is broken.

## The problem it solves

The SRA requires firms to publish price and service information for nine specified areas of work,
plus a complaints procedure explaining how and when to approach the Legal Ombudsman. A year after
the rules took effect, **three quarters of firm websites were non-compliant**. The SRA runs
proactive web sweeps; the fixed penalty is **£750** for a first finding and £1,500 after.

Most firms are *nearly* compliant. The gap is usually one paragraph, and they do not know which.

## Why anyone would trust it

Every finding quotes the firm's own wording and links the exact page, so it can be checked in ten
seconds. Findings that cannot be confirmed are reported as "worth checking", never as breaches.
This matters more than any other product attribute: the detection pipeline behind this has
produced five false positives in testing, each caught before it reached anyone, and the entire
value proposition dies the first time it tells a solicitor something untrue about their own site.

## What it is not

Not legal advice. Not a solicitor. Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to the
Solicitors Regulation Authority or the Legal Ombudsman — the domain name and copy deliberately
avoid implying otherwise, because implying affiliation with the regulator you are citing is passing
off.

## Surfaces

- **Landing page** (`index.html`) — Persuade. One page. The visitor arrives from a cold email that
  already told them something true about their own website; the page's job is to make an unknown
  sender look like a real, careful, accountable business, and to make £149 feel small against £750.

## Voice

Plain, specific, unhurried. States numbers rather than adjectives. Concedes what it does not know.
Never threatens; the regulator's penalty is context, not a stick.
